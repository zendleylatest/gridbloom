App Overview
Q: What is WiFi Horizon?
A: WiFi Horizon, also shown as WiFiHorizon: AI WiFi Analyzer, is a smart WiFi utility app that helps users understand their WiFi connection, monitor signal quality, scan nearby networks, test internet speed, review WiFi health, and get AI-powered troubleshooting guidance.

Q: What can I do with WiFi Horizon?
A: You can monitor your current WiFi connection, view SSID and local IP details, check signal strength, review band and channel information, scan nearby networks, run speed tests, view speed test history, ask the AI assistant WiFi questions, and manage app settings.

Q: Who is WiFi Horizon for?
A: WiFi Horizon is for users who want clearer WiFi diagnostics, easier troubleshooting, better signal awareness, and practical guidance for improving home, office, or personal network performance.

Q: Is WiFi Horizon only a speed test app?
A: No. WiFi Horizon includes speed testing, but it also provides signal monitoring, network details, nearby network scanning, WiFi health scoring, channel and band information, AI WiFi guidance, settings, profile features, and premium unlimited chat access.

Q: What makes WiFi Horizon useful?
A: WiFi Horizon turns technical WiFi information such as signal strength, frequency band, channel, link speed, network security, and scan results into simpler explanations and practical steps users can understand.

Q: Does WiFi Horizon fix WiFi problems automatically?
A: No. WiFi Horizon provides diagnostics and recommendations. Users may still need to restart equipment, move a router, change router settings, contact their internet provider, upgrade equipment, or check device-specific issues.

Q: Can WiFi Horizon guarantee faster internet?
A: No. The app can help identify likely issues and suggest improvements, but real speed depends on your internet plan, router, device hardware, distance, interference, congestion, ISP quality, and network setup.

Q: Can WiFi Horizon be used by beginners?
A: Yes. WiFi Horizon is designed to make WiFi information easier to understand, even for users who are not technical.

Q: Can WiFi Horizon be used by advanced users?
A: Yes. Advanced users can review signal values, frequency band, channel, nearby network details, security labels, speed test history, and AI explanations.

Home Screen and WiFi Health
Q: What is the Home screen used for?
A: The Home screen shows current WiFi connection information, WiFi health score, SSID, local IPv4 address, signal history, and quick access to network-related details.

Q: What does SSID mean?
A: SSID is the WiFi network name that users see when choosing a network.

Q: What does BSSID mean?
A: BSSID is a unique identifier for a WiFi access point, usually related to the router or access point radio. It may not always be shown depending on platform limitations.

Q: What is local IPv4?
A: Local IPv4 is the device's local network address assigned by the router, such as 192.168.x.x. It is used inside the local network.

Q: What is WiFi signal strength?
A: WiFi signal strength measures how strongly your device receives the wireless signal from the router or access point.

Q: What is RSSI?
A: RSSI stands for Received Signal Strength Indicator. It is commonly shown in dBm and helps estimate WiFi signal quality.

Q: What does dBm mean?
A: dBm is a signal strength unit. WiFi signal values are usually negative numbers, and values closer to zero are stronger.

Q: Is -50 dBm good?
A: Yes. Around -50 dBm is usually a strong WiFi signal.

Q: Is -60 dBm good?
A: Around -60 dBm is usually good for normal browsing, streaming, and calls.

Q: Is -70 dBm weak?
A: Around -70 dBm is fair to weak. It may work but can cause slower speeds, buffering, or unstable calls.

Q: Is -80 dBm bad?
A: Around -80 dBm is weak and may cause dropouts, slow speeds, or difficulty maintaining a stable connection.

Q: Why does WiFi signal show negative numbers?
A: WiFi signal is measured in dBm, where values are typically below zero. A value such as -50 dBm is stronger than -80 dBm.

Q: What is the WiFi health score?
A: The WiFi health score is a simplified rating that summarizes connection quality using signal strength and link speed information where available.

Q: How is the WiFi health score calculated?
A: The app maps RSSI roughly from weak to strong and lightly factors link speed to produce a score from 0 to 100.

Q: What do WiFi health labels mean?
A: Scores may be labeled Excellent, Good, Fair, Weak, or Poor to help users understand network quality quickly.

Q: What does Excellent WiFi health mean?
A: Excellent generally means the signal and connection quality look strong.

Q: What does Good WiFi health mean?
A: Good generally means the network should work well for normal use.

Q: What does Fair WiFi health mean?
A: Fair means the connection may work but could be affected by distance, interference, or congestion.

Q: What does Weak WiFi health mean?
A: Weak means users may experience slow speeds, buffering, lag, or drops.

Q: What does Poor WiFi health mean?
A: Poor means the device likely has a very weak or unreliable connection.

Q: Can the health score be wrong?
A: Yes. It is a helpful estimate based on available device data. Real-world performance can also depend on ISP speed, router load, device hardware, congestion, and server conditions.

Q: Why can my health score be high but internet still slow?
A: A strong WiFi signal only means the local wireless connection looks good. Slow internet can still be caused by ISP issues, low plan speed, router overload, remote server problems, VPNs, or many devices using bandwidth.

Q: Why can my speed be fast but signal score lower?
A: Short speed tests may still perform well even when signal is not ideal, especially if the router and internet plan are strong. Signal quality affects stability and consistency over time.

Channels, Bands, and Frequency
Q: What is a WiFi band?
A: A WiFi band is a frequency range used by wireless networks, such as 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, or 6 GHz.

Q: What is 2.4 GHz WiFi?
A: 2.4 GHz WiFi usually has longer range and better wall penetration, but it is often more crowded and slower than 5 GHz or 6 GHz.

Q: What is 5 GHz WiFi?
A: 5 GHz WiFi usually offers faster speeds and less congestion than 2.4 GHz, but it has shorter range and weaker wall penetration.

Q: What is 6 GHz WiFi?
A: 6 GHz WiFi is used by newer WiFi 6E and WiFi 7 equipment. It can offer high speeds and less congestion, but range may be shorter and device support is limited.

Q: Which band should I use?
A: Use 2.4 GHz for longer range and simple devices, 5 GHz for faster nearby connections, and 6 GHz if both router and device support it and you are close enough.

Q: What is a WiFi channel?
A: A WiFi channel is a smaller slice of a frequency band used by a router to communicate with devices.

Q: Why do channels matter?
A: If many nearby networks use the same or overlapping channels, interference and congestion can reduce performance.

Q: What channels are usually best on 2.4 GHz?
A: Channels 1, 6, and 11 are commonly recommended for 2.4 GHz because they do not overlap in many regions.

Q: Should I always manually change my WiFi channel?
A: Not always. Many routers handle channel selection automatically. Manual changes may help if auto selection performs poorly.

Q: Can WiFi Horizon change my router channel?
A: No. The app can help identify channel information and suggest ideas, but channel changes must be made in the router settings.

Q: What is frequency in MHz?
A: Frequency in MHz identifies the exact radio frequency being used. The app converts frequency into a band and channel where possible.

Q: Why does the channel show unknown?
A: The device or operating system may not provide frequency data, or the frequency may not map cleanly to a known channel.

Security and Network Safety
Q: What security types can WiFi Horizon show?
A: The app may show labels such as WPA3, WPA2, WPA, WEP, Open, or another security string depending on scan results.

Q: Is WPA3 secure?
A: WPA3 is currently one of the stronger common WiFi security standards when properly configured.

Q: Is WPA2 secure?
A: WPA2 is widely used and generally secure when paired with a strong password and updated router firmware.

Q: Is WPA outdated?
A: Older WPA is weaker than WPA2 and WPA3 and should be upgraded when possible.

Q: Is WEP secure?
A: No. WEP is obsolete and insecure. Users should switch to WPA2 or WPA3 if possible.

Q: Is an open WiFi network safe?
A: Open WiFi has no WiFi password protection and can expose traffic risks. Users should avoid sensitive activity on open networks unless using trusted protection such as HTTPS and a reputable VPN.

Q: Can WiFi Horizon guarantee that a network is safe?
A: No. The app can show indicators and provide guidance, but it cannot guarantee a network is safe or free from threats.

Q: Can WiFi Horizon detect hackers?
A: No. The app is not a full intrusion detection system. It may show network details and possible risk indicators, but it cannot prove hacking by itself.

Q: What should I do if I suspect someone is using my WiFi?
A: Check your router's connected devices list, change the WiFi password, use WPA2 or WPA3, disable WPS if not needed, update router firmware, and contact your ISP or router vendor if unsure.

Q: Should I share my WiFi password in AI chat?
A: No. Do not share WiFi passwords, router admin passwords, private keys, or sensitive credentials in chat.

Q: What does upload speed mean?
A: Upload speed measures how fast data goes from your device to the internet. It affects video calls, sending files, cloud backups, and live streaming.

Q: Why is download speed usually higher than upload speed?

Q: Why are speed test results different each time?

Q: Why is my WiFi speed lower than my internet plan?

Q: Why is speed faster near the router?

Q: Why is speed slower in another room?

Q: Why is speed slow even with full bars?
A: Full bars only estimate signal strength. Internet speed can still be limited by router load, ISP problems, congestion, device limits, or remote server issues.

Troubleshooting Weak WiFi
Q: How can I improve weak WiFi signal?
A: Move closer to the router, place the router centrally and higher, reduce walls and obstacles, avoid placing the router near metal or appliances, use 5 GHz for nearby speed, use 2.4 GHz for range, and consider mesh WiFi or an extender for large spaces.

Q: What should I do if WiFi keeps disconnecting?
A: Restart the router and device, check signal strength, move closer, forget and reconnect to the network, update router firmware, reduce interference, check if other devices disconnect too, and contact the ISP if the issue affects the whole network.

Q: What should I do if WiFi is connected but internet does not work?
A: Check if other devices have internet, restart router and modem, confirm the ISP is not down, disable VPN temporarily, forget and reconnect WiFi, check DNS settings, and contact the ISP if needed.

Q: What should I do if only one device has WiFi problems?
A: Restart that device, forget and reconnect to WiFi, update the device software, check VPN or private DNS settings, reset network settings if needed, and compare with another network.

Q: What should I do if all devices have WiFi problems?
A: Restart modem and router, check ISP status, inspect cables, test near the router, verify router lights, and contact your internet provider if the problem continues.

Q: How can I reduce WiFi interference?
A: Move the router away from microwaves, cordless phones, Bluetooth-heavy areas, thick walls, metal cabinets, and crowded electronics. Try a different channel or band if needed.

Q: Why does my WiFi slow down at night?
A: Evening congestion can happen when many people in the home or neighborhood use the internet at the same time. ISP congestion can also affect peak hours.

Q: Why is video calling unstable?
A: Video calls need stable latency, upload speed, and signal quality. Weak WiFi, upload congestion, router overload, or ISP issues can cause freezing and dropouts.

Q: Why do games lag on WiFi?
A: Games are sensitive to latency, jitter, and packet loss. Weak signal, congestion, background downloads, or router load can cause lag.

Q: How can I improve gaming on WiFi?
A: Use 5 GHz or 6 GHz near the router, reduce other traffic, move closer, restart the router, use Ethernet if possible, and avoid background downloads.

Q: How can I improve streaming quality?
A: Use a strong signal, choose 5 GHz near the router, reduce other bandwidth use, restart the router, check your internet plan speed, and place streaming devices closer to the access point.

Q: What is a WiFi dead zone?
A: A dead zone is an area where WiFi signal is too weak or unstable for reliable use.

Q: How can I fix a WiFi dead zone?
A: Reposition the router, add a mesh node, use a wired access point, reduce obstacles, change band or channel, or use a better router for larger coverage.

Q: Are WiFi extenders always good?
A: Extenders can help coverage but may reduce speed or add latency if poorly placed. Mesh systems or wired access points are often better for larger homes.

Router Placement and Setup
Q: Where should I place my router?
A: Place the router near the center of the area you want to cover, elevated, open, away from metal, thick walls, appliances, and corners.

Q: Should the router be on the floor?
A: No. A router usually performs better when placed higher, such as on a shelf or table.

Q: Should I hide the router in a cabinet?
A: No. Cabinets, especially metal or dense furniture, can weaken WiFi signal.

Q: Can walls affect WiFi?
A: Yes. Concrete, brick, stone, metal, mirrors, and thick walls can reduce WiFi signal.

Q: Can microwaves affect WiFi?
A: Yes. Microwaves can interfere with 2.4 GHz WiFi, especially when the router or device is nearby.

Q: Can Bluetooth affect WiFi?
A: Bluetooth can contribute to congestion in the 2.4 GHz band, although modern devices often manage coexistence well.

Q: Should I restart my router?
A: Restarting can help temporary router problems, memory issues, or stuck connections, but frequent restarts may indicate a deeper issue.

Q: How often should I replace my router?
A: It depends on performance, coverage, security updates, and device needs. Older routers may struggle with many devices or newer internet speeds.

Q: Should I update router firmware?
A: Yes. Router firmware updates can improve stability, performance, and security.

Q: Should I use the same WiFi name for 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz?
A: It depends. A shared name is convenient and lets the router steer devices. Separate names give users more manual control.

Q: What is band steering?
A: Band steering is a router feature that tries to move devices between 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, or 6 GHz based on performance and signal.

Q: What is mesh WiFi?
A: Mesh WiFi uses multiple nodes to provide broader coverage under one network name.

Q: Is mesh better than an extender?
A: Mesh often provides better roaming and coverage than basic extenders, but performance depends on placement, backhaul quality, and hardware.

Common WiFi Concepts
Q: What is a router?
A: A router connects devices to a local network and usually provides internet access through a modem or ISP connection.

Q: What is a modem?
A: A modem connects your home or office to the internet provider's network.

Q: What is an access point?
A: An access point provides WiFi coverage and connects wireless devices to a wired or router-managed network.

Q: What is an extender?
A: An extender repeats WiFi signal to improve coverage, but it may reduce speed depending on placement and technology.

Q: What is latency?
A: Latency is the delay between sending a request and receiving a response. It affects gaming, video calls, and responsiveness.

Q: What is ping?
A: Ping is a common measure of latency, usually shown in milliseconds.

Q: What is jitter?
A: Jitter is variation in latency. High jitter can make calls, gaming, and streaming feel unstable.

Q: What is packet loss?
A: Packet loss happens when data packets do not reach their destination. It can cause lag, dropouts, and poor call quality.

Q: What is bandwidth?
A: Bandwidth is the amount of data a connection can carry over time, often measured in Mbps.

Q: What is Mbps?
A: Mbps means megabits per second, a common unit for internet speed.

Q: What is throughput?
A: Throughput is the real data transfer rate achieved by a connection, which may be lower than the theoretical maximum.

Q: What is congestion?
A: Congestion happens when too many devices or networks compete for available bandwidth or radio space.

Q: What is interference?
A: Interference is unwanted radio noise or overlapping signals that can reduce WiFi performance.

Q: What is roaming?
A: Roaming is when a device moves between access points or mesh nodes while staying on the same network.

Q: What is DHCP?
A: DHCP is the system that automatically assigns local IP addresses to devices on a network.

Q: What is DNS?
A: DNS translates website names into IP addresses. DNS problems can make websites fail even when WiFi is connected.

Q: What is VPN?
A: A VPN routes traffic through a private tunnel. It can improve privacy but may also reduce speed or affect connectivity.

Q: What is WPS?
A: WPS is a router feature for easier device connection. It can be a security risk on some routers and may be disabled if not needed.

Q: What is QoS?
A: QoS stands for Quality of Service. It prioritizes certain traffic such as gaming, calls, or streaming on supported routers.

Q: What is WiFi 6?
A: WiFi 6 is a newer WiFi generation designed for better efficiency, speed, and performance with many devices.

Q: What is WiFi 6E?
A: WiFi 6E extends WiFi 6 into the 6 GHz band on supported routers and devices.

Q: What is WiFi 7?
A: WiFi 7 is a newer WiFi generation designed for higher throughput, lower latency, and improved performance on supported devices.
